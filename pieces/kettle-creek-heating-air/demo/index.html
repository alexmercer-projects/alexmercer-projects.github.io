A first batch of direct-response video ads for a local furnace tune-up offer, written to be shot on a phone by the crew, in a real basement, in one afternoon.

The brief

Offer
Fall furnace tune-up, flat $89, booked online or by text. placeholder price
Who it’s for
Homeowners 30–65 within a 25-minute drive, furnace 8+ years old, who haven’t had it looked at since last winter.
What stops them
“It’s working fine.” Fear of an upsell. Not wanting a stranger in the house on a weekday.
Job of the ad
Make a no-heat night feel close and specific, make the price feel safe, and make booking feel like one tap.
Guardrails
No fake urgency, no “limited spots” unless true, no scare stats without a source, no before/after claims we can’t back up.

Four angles

  1. A

    The 2 a.m. click

    The furnace quits on the coldest night, when every company charges after-hours rates.

  2. B

    Price, out loud

    Say the number in the first three seconds and say what isn’t included.

  3. C

    Show the dirt

    The filter, the burner, the flame sensor. Let the camera do the convincing.

  4. D

    The tech, not the van

    One named tech talking like a neighbor. Booties on, no pitch.

Hook bank

First 1–3 seconds. Each line is what’s said or shown on screen, with the opening shot underneath.

  1. A

    “Your furnace doesn’t care that it’s 2 a.m.”

  2. A

  8. C

  9. C

  12. D

    “Things I’d check if this were my mom’s house.”

    Tech walking down basement stairs, handheld.

How I’d test it

These ads haven’t run, so there are no numbers here. This is the plan for finding out which angle works.

  1. Week 1. One body (script 02’s middle), four different hooks, one from each angle. Same budget each, same audience.
  2. Read 3-second hold rate and cost per booking, not likes. Kill anything that loses on both after a fair spend.
  3. Week 2. Take the winning angle and shoot three more hooks from the bank in that lane.
  4. Refresh when cost per booking drifts up. Swap the hook first, the body last.
